Why it exists

An Australian employer answers to the ATO, a state revenue office, the FairWork Commission, a WHS regulator and several others at once, and each of them publishes its rules in a different place, in a different shape, changing on a different date. Accounting and payroll software applies the rules it has been given. Checking that those rules are the current ones, and that the figures they produce are the ones the law requires, has been a manual job, done from memory and a spreadsheet.

TobyAI does that checking. It holds the law and the published figures as one versioned, cited body, and it holds the tools that read your data and test it against them. Every result shows its working and names the provision it rests on, so the person signing off can see why, not just what.

Beside your software, not instead of it

Xero, MYOB and the payroll systems are good at what they do: recording transactions, reconciling, paying people on time and lodging through STP. TobyAI is not one of them and does not want to be. It keeps no ledger, runs no payroll and lodges nothing. It reads figures from your file or an export, read-only, checks them against the Act, the rate and the award clause in force on that date, and hands you the working and the citation.

The same is true of your accountant or registered agent. TobyAI does the finding and the arithmetic; the professional judgement and the sign-off stay with a person. The FAQ sets out the division in detail.

How a figure is made

Two layers, kept apart on purpose.

Every compliance figure is computed in auditable code from versioned data. AI explains a result and never originates a rate, a threshold, a date, a classification or a legal position.

The first layer is deterministic: rates, thresholds, caps and dates live in schedules that name their source and the date they were checked, and the engines compute from those in Python that can be read and re-run. The second layer is AI, used only to explain a computed result in plain language and to help you frame the question. It cannot change a figure or add a rate, and the code that holds that boundary is checked on every change.

Where a question needs a judgement before the arithmetic can run, whether someone is an employee or a contractor, whether a payment is a fringe benefit, TobyAI surfaces the test and its citation and stops. A person decides, and the decision is logged with who made it and what they saw.

Where TobyAI does not hold the source for a date or a jurisdiction, it says so rather than estimating. A refusal with a reason is a better answer than a plausible number.

The Compliance Lead

Every schedule TobyAI computes from is attested by a named Compliance Lead, a chartered accountant, before it can produce a figure for a customer. Attestation means the figures were checked against the publisher's own page, the source and the date were recorded, and the schedule was signed with a fingerprint the engine verifies each time it loads. When the ATO or a revenue office publishes a new rate, the change goes through the same attestation before it reaches you, and the old schedule is kept so an answer as at an earlier date still computes from the figures that applied then.

Independent AI review of code changes happens as well, but it is not treated as compliance sign-off. A model does not attest a rate; a person does.

Sydney, and what that means

TobyAI is built and run in Sydney. Compute, the database, the encrypted store for connection tokens and the operational logs run in the AWS Sydney region, and the AI models the assistant uses run in Australia on AWS Bedrock. Personal information never reaches an AI provider, and nothing you send is used to train a model. The Security and privacy page describes the boundary and how it is held.
