What TobyAI is

What is TobyAI?

Two things that only work together. One is a consolidated body of Australian compliance law, Commonwealth and state, from income tax, GST, PAYG and super through payroll tax and the FairWork awards to work health and safety, privacy and ASIC obligations, held as one versioned, cited corpus. The other is the tooling that reads your data and checks it against that law: data-quality checks over an accounting file, payroll and BAS reconciliations, obligation checklists, calculators and a research surface that answers from the provision. Every figure shows its working and cites the source it rests on.

It is not accounting software, not a payroll system and not a lodgement service. It is the thing you reach for when the question is whether the figure your software holds is the one the law requires.

Who is it for?

Bookkeepers and accountants who need to stand behind a number for a client, and the businesses they act for, especially those employing across state lines. If you have ever kept a spreadsheet of thresholds, rates and due dates beside your accounting software, TobyAI replaces the spreadsheet and adds the citation.

Is this tax, legal or financial advice?

No. TobyAI states what the legislation, the revenue office or the award provides, and what your figures compute to under it. That is information, not tax, legal, financial or employment advice, and the Terms of Use say so. A qualified person decides what to do with it. For a registered agent that means TobyAI does the finding and the arithmetic; the professional judgement, and the sign-off, stay with you.

How is this different from asking a chatbot, or searching the ATO site?

A general chatbot writes the number. TobyAI never does. Rates, thresholds, dates and caps live in versioned schedules that name their source and the date they were checked, and the engine computes from those in code. AI is used only to explain a computed result in plain language and to help you frame the question; it cannot change a figure, add a rate or take a legal position.

Searching the ATO or a revenue office finds you the page. TobyAI finds the provision, applies it to your figures, tells you which version of the law it used and gives you a record you can file. And where it does not hold the source, it says so rather than filling the gap.

Working with Xero, MYOB and payroll software

Is TobyAI a replacement for Xero or MYOB?

No. TobyAI keeps no ledger and writes nothing to yours. It reads figures from your accounting file or an export and checks them against the law in force on the date in question. Keep your accounting software; TobyAI sits beside it and does the part it was never built to do.

Does TobyAI run payroll?

No. It checks that the super guarantee rate, the PAYG scale, the payroll tax threshold, the due dates and the award rates your payroll uses are the ones the law provides on that pay date, and shows where the law says so. Paying people stays with your payroll system.

Does TobyAI lodge with the ATO or a revenue office?

No. TobyAI prepares and checks. You or your registered agent lodge, as you do today.

What does TobyAI read from Xero, and can it change anything?

Read-only accounting data, authorised by you in Xero, read server side in Sydney. The free health check reports what in the file would not survive review, with each finding cited. TobyAI never writes to your Xero file, and you can revoke its access in Xero at any time.

My clients use MYOB, QuickBooks or another payroll system. Does it still work?

Yes, by file. Export the report or payroll data from the software and attach it in the portal; the same engine audits it against the same sources and shows each finding with its citation. Xero connects directly today; direct connections to other ledgers and payroll systems follow.

How does it fit into a bookkeeping or accounting practice?

As the check before the figure goes out. Run a client's payroll period, BAS or year-end figures through it and get back the items that do not match the law in force, each with the provision, the working and the schedule version. That is the file note. Clients are served from the same portal, and the answer they receive is the one you would have had to look up.

Checking and verifying your data

What tooling is there, beyond looking up the law?

More than thirty tools, each one deterministic and each one citing what it rested on. They fall into four kinds:

  • Data health. Reads an accounting file and reports what would not survive review: duplicate contacts, dormant accounts, stale bank feeds, aged unreconciled transactions, contact ABNs that do not match the ABR.
  • Reconciliation and close. Payroll close, STP finalisation against the ledger, BAS label checks, TPAR, month-end close progress, receivables ageing.
  • Calculation. Payroll tax by state, super guarantee and payday readiness, FBT, termination and redundancy, depreciation, Div 7A, PSI, and a forward tax position, each from a versioned schedule that says whether it is attested.
  • Obligations and research. Due dates from the tax calendar, obligation checklists across privacy, AML/CTF, information security and ASIC director duties, and a research surface that answers a question from the provision itself.

The law and the tooling are the same product on purpose: a check is only worth running if it can tell you which provision it failed against.

What does the free Xero health check actually look at?

It reads the accounting data you authorise, read-only, and returns a list of findings rather than a score: duplicate and near-duplicate contacts, contact ABNs that fail the check digit or repeat across contacts, dormant accounts, missing or inconsistent tax codes, transactions coded to the wrong account, and transactions left unreconciled past their age. Each finding says what it found, where, and why it matters. Nothing is written back to your file.

Can I hand it a spreadsheet or an export instead of connecting anything?

Yes. Attach a spreadsheet, a payroll export or a document in the portal and the same engines audit it against the same primary sources. If the workbook cannot be read honestly, for instance the columns cannot be identified with confidence, it is refused rather than guessed at. That is how MYOB, QuickBooks and other payroll systems are supported today, while Xero connects directly.

How is this different from the checks my software already runs?

Your software checks itself against its own rules and its own tables. TobyAI checks it against the law: it re-derives the figure from the Act, the revenue office's published rate or the award clause on the date in question, and compares. When the two disagree, you get the difference, the provision and the working, not a warning icon.

What do I get at the end of a run?

The answer, the working in cents, the citation for each figure, the version of the schedule used and the date its source was checked. Every run is recorded, so months later you can show what was asked, what the law said then, and what the tool returned. That is the point of the audit trail: not that the tool was confident, but that its answer can be re-derived.

Will a tool ever give me a figure it cannot support?

No. A tool whose schedule is not attested, or whose source has not been published for the period asked, refuses and says which input it is missing. Judgement calls, whether entities are grouped, whether someone is a contractor, are put to you with the test and its citation, and the figure is computed only after you record the decision.

Where the figures come from

Where does a number come from?

From a versioned schedule that names its source (the Act, the ATO page, the revenue office page, the Fair Work Commission's data) and the date it was checked, attested by a named Compliance Lead before it is used. The engine reads the schedule and computes the figure with the working shown in cents. You can read the schedule and check it against the source yourself.

What does AI actually do, then?

It explains the computed answer in plain language and helps you ask the right question. It never originates a rate, threshold, date, classification or legal position, and the engine that computes figures contains no AI at all. That boundary is enforced in code and tested on every change, not stated as a policy.

How do I know a figure is right?

Three ways. The working is shown, so you can follow it. The source is cited and linked, so you can read the provision or the published rate yourself. And the engine is held to the worked examples the publishers print, so a Revenue NSW or SRO Victoria example computes to the publisher's own answer before a change ships. Each answer also states whether the schedule behind it is attested.

What about questions that are a judgement call?

Employee or contractor, whether entities are grouped, whether a position is reasonably arguable: TobyAI surfaces the test and its citation and stops. A person records the decision, and the evidence they saw, before any figure is computed on it. The decision is logged as an input, never inferred or pre-filled.

What happens when TobyAI does not have the answer?

It says so. A state it does not compute, a rate the ATO has not yet published, a compilation it does not hold for the date asked: each is refused, or handed back with the test and the citation, rather than filled with an estimate that looks right.

The law it holds

Which law does TobyAI hold?

One central hub for the obligations a business and its advisers have to meet, at Commonwealth and state level, across the whole compliance surface rather than one corner of it:

  • Commonwealth tax. Income tax, GST, PAYG withholding and instalments, super guarantee, FBT, Div 7A, PSI and the administration Acts, as Federal Register compilations, with the ATO's published rates, guidance and rulings beside them.
  • State and territory revenue. The payroll tax Act of every state and territory, the revenue offices' published rates, thresholds and worked examples, long service leave and workers' compensation Acts.
  • Employment. The Fair Work Act and the National Employment Standards, the FairWork awards with their classifications, rates and allowances, and the Fair Work Commission's published data.
  • The wider regulatory surface. Work health and safety Acts in each jurisdiction, the Privacy Act, AML/CTF, the Corporations Act and ASIC obligations, competition and consumer law, modern slavery, prudential and accounting standards.

More than three thousand documents, held as the published text with every paragraph traceable to its publisher, so a citation points at the provision as it stood, not a paraphrase. Bringing them into one place is the point: an obligation missed is usually one that lived in a source nobody was watching. What is computed, and what is held for research and citation only, is set out on the coverage pages.

Can I ask about a prior year?

Yes. Ask as at a date and TobyAI answers on the law in force then, naming the compilation or schedule it used. Amended returns, audits and late lodgements are answered on the law of the day, not today's rates.

How current is it?

Legislation and published rates are refreshed on a schedule against their publishers, and each document carries the date the publisher printed and the date it was retrieved. A change to a figure goes through the Compliance Lead's attestation before the engine uses it. Where a publisher has not yet printed a figure, such as a quarterly interest rate the ATO has not released, TobyAI says the figure is not yet held.

Which states and territories are covered?

Commonwealth law applies everywhere. Payroll tax computes for Queensland, New South Wales and Victoria today; the other states' and territories' Acts are held for research, and a question that needs a figure for one of them is refused rather than approximated.

Data and security

Where is my data, and my clients' data?

In Sydney, and it stays there. Compute, the database, the encrypted store for connection tokens and the operational logs all run in the AWS Sydney region, with data encrypted at rest and in transit. The AI models the assistant uses run in Australia too, on AWS Bedrock. The privacy policy sets this out.

What does AI actually see?

Never the personal information. Tax file numbers, bank account numbers, salary figures, employee names and raw organisation identifiers do not reach an AI provider. Before anything leaves the deterministic layer, identifiers are replaced with a salted hash unique to your organisation and the record is reduced to the non-personal shape the explanation needs: an account code, a label, a computed figure. That boundary is held by automated checks that run on every change to the code, not by a policy document.

Is my data used to train AI?

No. Nothing you send, upload or connect is used to train, fine-tune or otherwise update any model, ours or a provider's. AI runs as a fixed, measured model that receives a de-identified view at the moment it runs and retains nothing from it. Your data is not sold or shared with a third party for their own use.

Who can see my organisation's data?

The people you add to your organisation, and no other customer. Every record is siloed to your organisation; nothing is pooled across customers or shown to another. Sign-in is through your identity provider, connections such as Xero are read-only and revocable by you at any time, and the API refuses any tool call until the current Terms of Use have been accepted by your organisation.

Can I keep a record of what it told me?

Yes. Each answer carries the schedule version, the source, the provision and a fingerprint of the assessment, so a file note can be reconstructed later and the provision reread as it stood.
